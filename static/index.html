Security & Defense Demonstration

Security Testing & Attack Lab

See how KeyCockpit defends against real automotive attack vectors using genuine cryptographic verification.

Replay Attack

Analogy: Playing back a recorded tape

What the attacker is attempting: An eavesdropper recorded the radio signal when Alice unlocked her car earlier, and now attempts to broadcast that exact recorded signal again to break in.

How KeyCockpit protects: Every interaction requires a fresh, single-use random "challenge" from the vehicle. Once used, that challenge is permanently destroyed. Replaying old messages fails.

ℹ️ Unlock the car on the Dashboard once first so the eavesdropper captures a message to replay.

Relay Attack

Analogy: Two walkie-talkies whispering across town

What the attacker is attempting: Alice's real phone is far away inside her house. An attacker with a signal repeater forwards the car's challenge to her phone and relays the genuine response back.

How KeyCockpit protects: Distance bounding via response-time measurement. Relaying signals over a distance causes delay. The car enforces a 150 ms deadline; late answers are rejected.

Ready to test.

Forged Digital Key

Analogy: Showing a fake homemade ID badge

What the attacker is attempting: An attacker generates their own digital key claiming "Owner" status, signed by their own unauthorized fake authority.

How KeyCockpit protects: The car only trusts certificates signed by the vehicle owner's official issuer key. Cryptographic math makes forging an Ed25519 signature computationally impossible.

Ready to test.

Copied Certificate

Analogy: Finding a lost ID badge without the PIN

What the attacker is attempting: Digital certificates are public. An attacker copies Bob's valid certificate over the air and tries to unlock the car with it.

How KeyCockpit protects: A certificate alone cannot unlock the car. The phone must also sign the car's random challenge with the matching secret private key (which only Bob's real phone has).

Ready to test.

KeyCockpit Glossary — Technical vs User-Friendly Terms

      
    


    

      
        

          Technical Concept
          User-Friendly Meaning
        

      
      
        

          Certificate
          Digital Key — A cryptographically signed digital pass stored securely inside the smartphone.
        

        

          Authentication
          Identity Verification — Proving the phone really belongs to the authorized driver without sending secrets over the air.
        

        

          Authorization
          Permission Check — Checking if this key's role (Owner, Guest, Valet) permits this command.
        

        

          Public Key
          Phone Identification Key — A shareable ID that the car uses to mathematically verify signatures.
        

        

          Private Key
          Secret Phone Key — A private mathematical key that NEVER leaves the phone hardware.
        

        

          Revocation
          Disable Digital Key — Blacklisting a key so the car permanently refuses it.
        

        

          Challenge-Response
          Security Verification — A single-use math puzzle that completely stops recording/replay attacks.
